Scene 02 of 15 from the full one-act. Stage directions in the margins, lyrics in the light.

(Characters: Quinn, Mia, Kimmy, Ron, G, Claudia — Setting: The coffeehouse on the first floor of the office building. Starts exterior, ends interior. Exterior. Coffee shop and office building. Morning. The Miami heat is already thick. The building has two street-level doors: COFFEE SHOP and OFFICE LOBBY. Between them, on the sidewalk, CLAUDIA sits at a small folding table, a guitar beside her. A half-eaten sandwich wrapped in foil rests beside her. MIA, G, and QUINN approach. QUINN walks slightly apart, shrugging her work bag higher on her shoulder, scanning for something, and putting up her hair. She stops outside the coffee shop.)

QUINN

(resigned) No sign of him.

MIA

The Llama?

G

Does Llama have any distinguishing markings?

MIA

It's a llama. In Coconut Grove. It's not like a peacock.

G

Ok! Looking for Llama the llama - and no peacocks.

MIA

(changing the subject) Can I get you a coffee, Quinn?

QUINN

No. I'm gonna be late, and I brought a new flavor for the office. It's the little things…

Besides, Chad likes to livestream my desk if he gets in before me.

G

Why?

QUINN

Showing off his five to nine before his nine to five. He'll have me updating his LinkedIn all day. See you in there!

(She starts toward the office lobby. CLAUDIA looks up from her guitar.)

CLAUDIA

Well, hey there, Miss Quinn.

(QUINN stops briefly to hug CLAUDIA.)

QUINN

Good morning! I'm running late, but lunch date?

(CLAUDIA holds up a foil-wrapped sandwich.)

CLAUDIA

Brought an extra sandwich for Llama.

QUINN

(genuinely touched, still walking backward) You're so thoughtful. See you at lunch!

CLAUDIA

(smiling) I'll be here!

(QUINN pushes through the office door. It closes behind her. MIA and G stand at the coffee-shop entrance, watching the interaction with curiosity. Through the window, they can see RON at a corner table, laptop open, an iced coffee already sweating a ring onto the wood. He looks up, sees them, and raises a hand in a lazy wave. MIA waves back—smaller, a little tight.)

G

(quietly, to MIA) Want thirty seconds before we go in?

MIA

I'm good.

(She breathes and looks at G.)

I promise. Thank you for coming with me.

G

You got this! You're the star!

MIA

(with a bigger smile) You're the best. Let's go - I think that's Ron over there.

G

You said Kimmy was working right?

MIA

(suspicious) Yea, why?

(G beams. MIA pushes through the coffee-shop door. G follows. The shop is small, warm, and already humming: exposed brick, too many laptops, one slow ceiling fan. RON sits at his table in the corner, scrolling. MIA and G start toward him. Then the lights shift. A single spotlight cuts down center stage. The rest of the shop dims to half-light, still visible and still alive, but suddenly secondary. KIMMY stands there on roller skates, a tray balanced on one hand and a coffee pot in the other. She does not look at the audience. She looks at the room she has already memorized. Singers: Kimmy, Ron, G, Mia.)

KIMMY House roast, double shot
Another kid with a screenplay plot
Oat milk, extra foam
Everybody here far away from home
Big dreams, low paychecks
Poets living off American Express
Tip jar, unpaid tab
Future genius washing out in the back

I used to think this room meant something
Sticky floors and broken air conditioning
Now everybody's building something scalable
Nobody's building a life

[VERSE 1 — SHE SKATES A SLOW CIRCLE, SEEING EVERYTHING]

KIMMY Drip coffee and day-old scones
Some guy crying into headphones
Girl by the window writing captions
About "healing" for a fashion brand

Every table's got a startup
Every startup's got a deck
Everybody's reinventing culture
Nobody can pay their check

(During the verse, MIA and G settle at RON's table. KIMMY skates past them—not looking at them yet, simply cataloging the room. MIA flinches slightly at "healing for a fashion brand." That is her. RON grins, amused.)

MIA

(quietly, to G) That one's a little personal.

KIMMY

(without breaking stride or looking over) You're sitting in a coffee shop in Miami. Everything here is personal.

[CHORUS — BUILDING; THE SHOP'S AMBIENT NOISE BECOMES PERCUSSION]

KIMMY And the coffee keeps dripping
Drip-drip-dripping all day
Everybody wants to make something
Nobody wants to stay

The coffee keeps dripping
Drip-drip-dripping all night
Everybody wants to be an artist
Until the rent comes due on the first of July

(At "first of July," KIMMY skates past RON's table again. This time she really looks at them. G opens his mouth to say something; she holds up one finger, still singing, not unkindly: not yet.)

G

(half-sung, half-muttered) But we're different, though. We've got an actual voice—

RON

(overlapping, smooth) Which is exactly why we automate the process.

KIMMY

(landing the beat, finally looking directly at them) And there it is. The two genders of tech bro.

[VERSE 2 — FASTER; SHE WEAVES BETWEEN TABLES]

KIMMY I moved here at nineteen
Thought I'd write the great American scene
Open mics and cigarettes
Cheap apartments and bad sex

Now the poets all do branding
And the punks all work in UX
And every revolutionary in this city
Has a podcast and a hex code set

(She skates past a customer in headphones who does not look up. She does not slow down.)

MIA

(more quietly, to G) Okay, that one was actually good.

G

You should post that somewhere.

KIMMY

(spinning on her skates without breaking the song) No. Some thoughts deserve to die in public.

[CHORUS]

KIMMY And the coffee keeps dripping
Drip-drip-dripping in lines
Everybody says they're searching
Mostly they're just buying time

The coffee keeps dripping
Drip-drip-dripping again
Everybody wants community
Until somebody asks them to commit

[BRIDGE — SHE STOPS; THE SHOP KEEPS MOVING AROUND HER]

KIMMY I watch people fall in love here
Three weeks later they don't speak
I watch painters become content farms
By the second cold brew of the week

I watch lonely people outsource talking
To whatever tells them "you're enough"
I watch dreamers slowly trade their soul away
For better metrics and easier stuff

And honestly? Sometimes I get it.

Tips are better now.

(During the bridge, the lights remain on RON's table. He has leaned forward and is already pitching in a low voice; we do not hear the words yet. G shakes his head. MIA watches KIMMY rather than RON, something complicated in her face.)

RON

You're thinking too small. One painting becomes ten thousand assets.

G

Or maybe art doesn't need to become content sludge to matter.

MIA

Can we stop flirting through ideology for, like, six seconds?

[FINAL CHORUS — KIMMY SNAPS BACK INTO MOTION]

KIMMY And the coffee keeps dripping
Drip-drip-dripping the same
Everybody came here certain
They were gonna change the game

The coffee keeps dripping
Drip-drip-dripping nonstop
Everybody swore they'd make real art
Now they're working on prompts
In a coffee shop

[OUTRO — SHE SLOWS AND LANDS]

KIMMY House roast, double shot
Another genius slowly getting bought
Oat milk, extra foam
Nobody here ever really goes home

(Blackout on KIMMY. The shop lights return to full. She stands beside RON's table, skates still on, her tray tucked under her arm as though it grew there. The song is over. The room is only a coffee shop again.)

KIMMY

(to the table; all business) What are we drinking?

RON

(already back in pitch mode, barely looking up) Iced oat latte. Extra ice. I'm here for a while.

G

(smiling, excitable) Just coffee. Black. Whatever's oldest.

MIA

...Matcha. If you have it.

KIMMY

(writing nothing down, already turning) We have it. We have everything. That's the problem.

(She skates toward the bar, then looks back once, only at MIA. KIMMY is already behind the counter. The steam wand hisses. MIA turns to RON as he slides a folder across the table.)

RON

(opening the folder without looking up) So. You said you wanted to talk scale.

(G looks at MIA. MIA looks at the folder. She does not touch it yet.)
