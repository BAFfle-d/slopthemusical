Scene 02 of 15 from the full one-act. Stage directions in the margins, lyrics in the light.

(Exterior. Coffee shop and office building. Morning. The Miami heat is already thick. The building has two street-level doors: COFFEE SHOP and OFFICE LOBBY. Between them, on the sidewalk, CLAUDE sits at a small folding table, working on watercolor sketches. A half-eaten sandwich wrapped in foil rests beside him. MIA, G, and QUINN approach. QUINN walks slightly apart, shrugging her work bag higher on her shoulder, scanning for something, and putting up her hair. She stops outside the coffee shop.)

QUINN

(resigned)

No sign of him.

MIA

Llama again?

G

I'll keep watch. Does he have any distinguishing markings?

MIA

It's a llama. In Coconut Grove. It's not like a peacock.

G

Does it have a name?

QUINN

(sadly)

He. Llama is a he. His name is Llama.

G

Perfect. I'll report any llama. No peacocks.

MIA

(changing the subject)

Are you going to get a coffee with us?

QUINN

No. I'm gonna be late, and I have a new Christmas blend I want to try, anyways.

G

Just thinking of Christmas makes me excited for sweater season!

QUINN

Exactly! I'll see you upstairs. If I'm not there when Chad walks in, he'll livestream my desk and claim that getting there first is part of his hustle. Then I'll be writing LinkedIn posts for him about his five-to-nine before his nine-to-five all day. He's done that twice this month. Bye!

(She starts toward the office lobby. CLAUDE looks up from his painting.)

CLAUDE

Well, hey there, Miss Quinn. You look ready to face a firestorm.

(QUINN stops briefly to hug CLAUDE.)

QUINN

Hey—I'm late. I'm sorry. I'll be back down later, I promise. Lunch date?

(CLAUDE holds up a foil-wrapped sandwich.)

CLAUDE

Brought three. Just in case the llama came along.

QUINN

(genuinely touched, still walking backward)

You're so thoughtful.

CLAUDE

(smiling)

Be good up there, Miss Quinn.

(QUINN pushes through the office door. It closes behind her. MIA and G stand at the coffee-shop entrance, watching the interaction with curiosity. Through the window, they can see RON at a corner table, laptop open, an iced coffee already sweating a ring onto the wood. He looks up, sees them, and raises a hand in a lazy wave. MIA waves back—smaller, a little tight.)

G

(quietly, to MIA)

Want thirty seconds before we go in?

MIA

I'm good.

(She breathes and looks at G.)

I promise. Thank you for coming with me.

G

Okay. I'm here for you.

MIA

(with a bigger smile)

Come on, G. You're the best.

G

You said Kimmy was working right?

MIA

(suspicious)

Yea, why?

(G beams. MIA pushes through the coffee-shop door. G follows. The shop is small, warm, and already humming: exposed brick, too many laptops, one slow ceiling fan. RON sits at his table in the corner, scrolling. MIA and G start toward him. Then the lights shift. A single spotlight cuts down center stage. The rest of the shop dims to half-light, still visible and still alive, but suddenly secondary. KIMMY stands there on roller skates, a tray balanced on one hand and a coffee pot in the other. She does not look at the audience. She looks at the room she has already memorized.)
